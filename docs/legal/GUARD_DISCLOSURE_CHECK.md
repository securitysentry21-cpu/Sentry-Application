# Guard disclosure check

> **DRAFT for lawyer review. Not legal advice. Date: 2026-11-08.**
> Prepared by an AI coding assistant. It compares the words a guard sees in the app with what the code really does and what the specifications promise. **The app has not been changed.** Suggested wording below is a suggestion only. All Urdu lines need a native speaker (the author reads Urdu but is not a native speaker). Open points are marked `[OWNER TO CONFIRM: ...]` and `[LAWYER: ...]`.

**What was compared**

- The guard-facing text: `apps/mobile/src/core/i18n/disclosure.ts` (tracking notice, version `1`), `en.ts`, `ur.ts`, and the iOS usage strings in `apps/mobile/app.config.ts`.
- What the phone sends: `packages/contracts/src/mobile.ts`, `apps/mobile/src/core/api/client.ts`, `apps/mobile/src/platform/*`, `apps/mobile/src/core/tracking/device-status.ts`.
- What the server stores and keeps: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §6.3, `apps/api/src/services/retention.ts`, `docs/DECISIONS.md` (Phase 9).
- What the specs promise: `docs/PRODUCT_SPEC.md` §3.2, §7.2, §8.1, §11, §16, Appendix B; `docs/SECURITY_AND_INVARIANTS.md` §16.

**Result in one line:** the tracking notice is accurate about _when_ location is collected and mostly about _why_. It is **incomplete on who sees it, what exactly is collected, how long it is kept, and what else the app records.** There is one claim to check on a device (the unremovable notification) and one purpose (emergencies) that is not available to pilot guards.

## 5. Order of events and the Google Play disclosure rule

- **M-10: data is sent before the notice.** The flow is: language → enrollment (code and mobile number, plus phone and app details, sent to the server) → tracking notice → permissions (`PRODUCT_SPEC.md` §4.3). The notice comes before any _location_ permission request, as `PRODUCT_SPEC.md` §7.2 requires, but not before the first data leaves the phone. `[LAWYER: is that acceptable, or should the first screen say what enrollment sends?]`
- **M-11: Google's "prominent disclosure" rule for background location.** Google asks for an in-app disclosure, shown in normal use, that says what location is collected and why, **and that it is collected when the app is closed or not in use**, with a clear user action, immediately before the permission request. Here, the notice comes earlier (at first launch), and the closest text to the permission request is `perm.backgroundExplain` ("also when the screen is off") on the readiness card. Neither uses "app is closed or not in use". `[OWNER TO CONFIRM: re-read Google's current policy text before the submission (EXT-45) and decide whether to add the sentence to the notice and to the explanation shown before "Allow all the time". Review rounds are expected (EXT-05).]`

## 6. The new crash-report feature

The guard app is gaining scrubbed error reports sent to our own API only. Fields: kind, error type name, source-file:line:column frames of our own code, app version, OS version, platform, fatal flag, timestamp, device ID and organization ID as opaque IDs. Written only to the service log, kept 30 days. No message text, names, numbers, locations or incident text. Source: owner decision (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, Phase 10) and the build brief of 2026-11-08.

The tracking notice does not mention it. The privacy policy does (`PRIVACY_POLICY.md` section 3.3). **Suggestion:** add one sentence to the notice (see section 8, S-8). `[OWNER TO CONFIRM: check the field list against the finished code before this is published.]`
